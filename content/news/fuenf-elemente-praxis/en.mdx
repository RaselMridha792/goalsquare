## The rule: two focuses, three touches

Fitting all five elements into 75 minutes sounds like too much. It works if you set two elements as the **focus** and only **touch** the other three – briefly, deliberately, with no claim to perfection.

## The structure

**0–12 min · Basic technique.** Activation, basic stance, hand-eye coordination with two balls. No pressure, clean execution.

**12–30 min · Footwork (focus).** Ladder, cones, lateral shifting. The last six minutes with a ball and a finish so the legs have to make decisions under load.

**30–50 min · Positioning (focus).** Bisecting the angle, shifting on ball movement, then crosses from the half-space. This is the actual core of the session.

**50–60 min · Ball protection.** Falling technique and shot stopping from a standing start, kept short – the legs are already tired at this point, and that is intentional.

**60–75 min · Game play.** Keeper vs. keeper on two goals, followed by a short final game as a cool-down.

## Why this order

Technique before strength, decisions before fatigue, competition at the end. Put the game forms at the start and you get a good mood and very little learning.
