## Why crosses?

No other passage of play leads to a conceded goal as often as the badly read cross. Yet in goalkeeper training it is regularly addressed too late and without structure – usually as a loose series of deliveries with no clear progression.

## How the series is built

The 24 new drills follow a fixed progression over four stages:

1. **Isolated.** Cross without an opponent, focus on the take-off leg and the timing of the starting point.
2. **With a passive opponent.** The keeper learns to get the body between ball and attacker.
3. **Active opponent.** Competing for the ball, first decisions: come out or hold the line.
4. **Game form.** Crosses out of live play, with a finish and rest defence.

## Coaching points

Every drill contains three to five coaching points. The most important ones deliberately repeat across all 24 drills: **leave the starting position on the line before the ball is struck**, switch the eyes between ball and space, and attack with the front foot first.
